DSP Acoustic Remediation Tool

Hearing acoustic treatment before it is installed: measure a room, simulate absorption and diffusion, then auralize.

Role
Developer - Final Project for Acoustics & Psychoacoustics
Advisor
Dr. Hubert Ho
Tools
MATLAB (Signal Processing Toolbox, Audio Toolbox), MaxMSP (HISS Tools)
Timeline
Mar 2026 – Apr 2026

Problem

After building my Acoustic Measurement and Auralization Toolkit, which captures and analyzes a room's impulse response, my next curiosity was if you could computationally simulate what the room would sound like after installing acoustic treatment without physically installing anything.

In architectural acoustics consulting, this is a real workflow, as an acoustician measures a space, identifies problems (too much reverberation, poor speech clarity, specular flutter echoes), and propose treatments (absorption panels, diffusers). The challenge is that clients can't hear the proposed improvement until it's built. I wanted to build something that solves that problem and closes that gap, meaning to measure a room, simulate the treatment in software, and generate an auralization so you can listen to the improvement.

Method

The whole system is a three-stage MATLAB pipeline: pre-processing, acoustic treatment simulation, and auralization.

Stage 1: IR Pre-Processing and Analysis

This first script starts by phase-aligning four rotational source measurements to artificially recreate an omnidirectional impulse response, compensating for the directional limitations of a non-ideal (non-omni) sound source. The combined IR is then analyzed per ISO 3382, using Schroeder backward integration across octave bands (125 Hz – 4 kHz) to extract T20 (extrapolated to RT60) and C50, establishing the baseline acoustic metrics of the untreated room.

Stage 2: DSP Treatment

Next, two acoustic treatment types are modeled:

Absorption: The IR is split into three frequency bands using crossover filters. Each band receives an independent exponential decay envelope (e^(-t/τ)), where the time constant τ is tuned to simulate the frequency-dependent absorption characteristics of real treatment materials, which means stronger attenuation at high frequencies (like fiberglass panels), weaker at low frequencies (where bass trapping is harder). The bands are recombined into a treated IR with a shorter, frequency-shaped decay.

Diffusion: To simulate diffusion, the script auto-detects specular reflections in the IR using peak detection, then scatters them through Schroeder all-pass filters with prime delay lines. This simulates the effect of diffuser panels, which breaks up focused reflections into a more uniform reverberant field without removing energy.

Stage 3: Auralization

The treated IR is convolved with anechoic recordings to generate before-and-after auralizations. This allows the listener to hear the room with and without the simulated treatments. This is the same convolution reverb used in the Auralization Toolkit, but now applied to computationally modified IRs rather than raw measured ones.

Result

The final system produces measurable improvements in both T20 and C50 after simulated treatment, with the absorption model reducing reverberation time and the diffusion model redistributing specular energy. The before/after auralizations make the difference audible, with treated sounds noticeably clearer and less reverberant. This project extends the pipeline I built in the Auralization Toolkit into a full measurement → analysis → simulation → auralization workflow.
